Counting visits

This site counts nothing about its readers. No page sends a visit, a click, a search or a place to anyone, except through the two choices below, and no page loads a counting service. Your settings, your companion and any place worked out for “Use my location” stay in this browser. Two choices of yours do reach other servers: if you switch on street maps on a ballot or Forecasts page, OpenStreetMap’s servers see which map squares are asked for; and if you type your address on a ballot or Forecasts page and press Look up, it goes from your browser straight to the U.S. Census Bureau’s address finder, which sends back where it is. This site never sees that address or keeps it. And the opening picture of Plain Congress asks Open-Meteo for the weather at the Capitol; that request carries the Capitol’s place, never yours. If counting is ever switched on, this section will say exactly what is counted, and two switches will turn it off.

This site does not count you. No page sends anything about you to anyone, except for two choices. Those two do reach other sites. Part 3 of 9 · About 1 minute

Sharing

A Share button sends a link to a page, or to the part of it beside the button. On a phone it opens your phone’s own share sheet. On a computer it opens a short list: copy the link, e-mail, Facebook, LinkedIn, X, Bluesky, WhatsApp or Reddit. Nothing is sent until you pick a place. The place you pick then sees the link and the short message with it, which says only what the page is about. Every place in the list is a plain link that opens in a new tab: no button, widget or script from those services is loaded on these pages, and this site never learns whom you share with. A shared link never carries the place this site worked out for you or an address you typed. Where a link would show roughly where you vote, such as your own ballot, the page says so first and asks.

The app

This site can be kept on your phone, tablet or computer as an app, installed from the site itself. Get the app shows how. Installing sends nothing to The Civic Archive. On Android, your browser’s maker (Google, for Chrome) builds the app from this site’s public description of itself; nothing you have read here is part of it.

You can keep this site on your phone as an app. Getting it sends nothing to this site.

To work with no connection, your browser keeps copies of the pages you open on this site, and of the small files they use, on this device: up to about 60 MB, the oldest making room for the newest. The Congress pages keep their own copies of the bills, votes and portraits you open, apart from that 60 MB. It never keeps the scenes on the way in, the places to sit or Election Night’s live figures. It also keeps, in this browser only, a count of your visits, whether you closed the note about the app, and whether you installed it here, so the note appears once and the top bar knows you have the app. None of it is ever sent anywhere. The app page shows how much is kept and clears it with one button; a ballot you kept is forgotten on its own page.

To work with no signal, your browser keeps copies of pages you open. They stay on this device. It also counts your visits here, so the note about the app shows only once. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Part 6 of 9 · About 2 minutes

What is done

On the front door, the Method page and this page:

On these three pages:

  • A “Skip to content” link is the first thing the keyboard reaches.
  • One header, one main area and one footer, with every navigation area named.
  • A visible focus ring on everything the keyboard can reach. No outline is taken away.
  • Every button, menu item and link on its own is at least 44 by 44 pixels. A link inside a sentence is the size of its words.
  • Nothing works by hovering alone. The menus open with a click, a tap or the keyboard, and Esc closes them.
  • No single-key shortcuts, so voice control cannot set one off by accident.
  • Twelve settings and eight presets, which combine. They are kept on this device and nowhere else.
  • Six levels, from Beginner to Archivist, set how much each page shows, and a Show more button stands wherever something is put away. The facts are the same at every level.
  • A device set to reduce motion is honoured on the first visit, before anything is touched.
  • The fade at the edges of the screen is off under high contrast and reduced motion, replays the same way scrolling up as down, and never dims a section parked in the middle of the screen.
  • The six icons are fully drawn even when no script runs, and every one of their moves ends at rest.
  • The chart never puts a number on a bar, has a table version, and adds patterns under the colour-vision palettes, Calm and high contrast. Its colours hold at least 3 to 1 against every background in light, dark and high contrast, and stay apart for three kinds of colour blindness, checked by simulation.
  • The companion is hidden from screen readers. Its control is a real button named “Open help”. Off loads nothing. Still draws it once.
  • The pages reflow to 320 pixels wide without scrolling sideways.

Part 7 of 9 · About 1 minute

What is not done yet

  • On the rest of the site (Plain Congress, the state pages, On The Ballot, Election Night’s results pages and the ring of cards) these carry across: the levels, text size, the two easier typefaces, spacing, high and dark contrast, Calm and motion. There, Reading & access, the button with two sliders beside each page’s own Motion switch (on a phone sometimes in the bottom left corner), changes them. Chart colours, the scroll fade, plain words and Detail work only on this page, the home page and the Method page. On those other pages a larger text size makes the words bigger but not the maps, the top bar or the largest headlines, and an easier typeface leaves the site’s name and the largest headlines as they are. The cabin and the places to sit keep only motion and the companion.
  • At 200 percent text, a few controls on those other pages can run past the edge of a phone’s screen.
  • How much each page shows (the levels) is being added page by page. Where a page has nothing marked yet, every level shows it as Medium does.
  • No one has yet walked through the site with a screen reader such as VoiceOver or NVDA. Automated checks find only part of what stops people.
  • Plain-language versions exist only for the main text of the front door, this page and the Method page. Their reading level is checked each time the pages are built.
  • The pages have not been timed on a four-year-old phone.
  • The vote map and the district maps on other pages have not been checked against this list.
  • This site makes no claim yet to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines.

The companion

The animal at the corner of the screen is optional. Its only job is to open Help, which is also in the top bar. It never speaks on its own and never makes a sound. It steps aside while you scroll, unless motion is reduced or it is Still. With motion on, pointing at it or reaching it with the keyboard makes it do a short dance made of the real animal's own moves. Choose it, still it or switch it off under Help or in the settings above.

The animal in the corner only opens Help. Point at it and it does a little dance. You can stop it or turn it off.

The seven animals are drawn on your own device with three.js (MIT licence). Each is sculpted in code from studies of how the real animal is built and how it moves. The snail's shell is shaped from a scan of a real shell by TinyWorlds, given to the public domain (CC0) at OpenGameArt.

Plain answers

Who pays for this site?

Nobody pays to appear on it. The site runs no advertising of its own and has no donors. It is published on GitHub's free public hosting.

Does this site track me?

This site counts nothing about its readers: no page sends a visit, a click or a place anywhere, except as described below. There is no account and no sign-in. Your reading settings and your companion are kept in this browser, on this device, and are never sent anywhere.

Where a page offers "Use my location", your place is worked out on your device and is not sent anywhere. Every page takes its type from this site. Three things come from other servers. The opening picture of Plain Congress shows the weather at the Capitol in Washington, from Open-Meteo; that request carries the Capitol's place, never yours. If you switch on street maps on a ballot or Forecasts page, OpenStreetMap's servers see which map squares are asked for, and the page says so. And if you type your address on a ballot or Forecasts page and press Look up, your browser sends it straight to the U.S. Census Bureau's address finder, which sends back where it is; this site never sees the address or keeps it.

What happens when I share a page?

A Share button sends a link. On a phone it opens your phone’s own share sheet; on a computer, a short list of places, such as e-mail, Facebook or LinkedIn, or Copy link. Nothing is sent until you pick a place. The place you pick then sees the link and the short message with it. This site loads nothing from those services and never learns whom you share with. If a link would show roughly where you vote, the page says so first. The Access page says more.

Can I get it as an app?

Yes. You can keep this site on your phone, tablet or computer, installed from the site itself: free, with no app store and no account, and it updates itself. Pages you have opened, and a ballot you kept, still work with no connection. Installing sends nothing to this site; on Android, your browser’s maker (Google, for Chrome) builds the app from the site’s public description of itself. Get the app shows how.

Why should I trust the ratings?

You do not have to take them on trust. Facts come from official records and link back to them. Ratings are judgments, kept apart and labelled. Each one shows its evidence grade, its reasons, its sources and the version of the rubric it was made under.

So far ten bills carry a full set of ratings. They were applied by hand, by Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, as a preview, and each says so. Who backed a bill is worked out from the recorded votes by a published formula. The Method page has the whole rubric.

It is here to open this guide, which changes with the page you are on. Every companion gives the same help. It never speaks on its own, and screen readers pass over it. Help stays in the top bar either way.
