Forecasts, race by race

Analysis A computer model’s estimate, never a result.

A forecast is a computer’s best guess about how a race may turn out. It is not a result: the vote count decides. Each one gives a chance of winning and a likely share of the vote. How the forecasts are made.

A forecast is this site’s own estimate of how a race may turn out, worked out by a computer model from past election results and from polls by members of the American Association for Public Opinion Research’s Transparency Initiative. It gives each leading candidate a chance of winning and a likely range of the vote, and shows how the model did when it was tested on past elections. It is Analysis: not a poll, not a call and not a result. The official count decides. How the forecasts are made.

How the model has done, at the foot of the page, gives its track record for each kind of race.

How the model has done Analysis

    A 70% chance means the candidate won 7 in every 10 of the model’s simulated elections, and lost the other 3. The likely range holds 8 in 10 of its simulated outcomes. No race has a forecast with only one name on the ballot.

    On election night the model runs again as the count comes in: Election Night’s forecasts show every run, with the inputs and the track record.

    Every run of the forecasts is also kept unchanged, stamped with its date and time, in the forecast record, to be checked against the official results.
