# The garden snail (Cornu aspersum): models and motion

Research notes for whoever builds the snail companion. Written 2026-10-03. Everything below is either quoted from a
source (with its address), derived from one (the arithmetic is shown), or marked **judgement** where no measured source
was found. A snail has no skeleton, so "articulation" here means the few things that really move: the foot's sole, the
head and neck, the four tentacles, the shell on its muscle, the breathing pore, and the whole animal in and out of its
shell.

Two scale facts to hold on to: an adult Cornu is 25–40 mm across the shell, 25–35 mm tall at the shell, with a head
and foot 50–60 mm long when extended ([Wikipedia, Cornu aspersum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornu_aspersum)); it
weighs 1.4–10 g, of which the shell is about a tenth ([Pardo-Sarmiento et al. 2026](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0354962):
total mass 1.37–10.32 g, median 3.48; shell 0.09–1.86 g, median 0.38). In the rig's units (1 = height to the top of
the shell, about 33 mm), the extended foot is 1.6–1.8 long and the shell 0.9–1.1 across.

### The shell scan, in detail

Downloaded (honest User-Agent, no account) to the session scratchpad, then converted. Original zip SHA-256
`544e647c39d761f3b9601530ca65557da2c67e7c6e0f6d4a4b708881db768cbd`. Converted file:
`shell_src/companions/models/snail/shell.glb` (643,856 bytes; SHA-256
`29d0863155ee646624ea50fc7ab81a3098bcb9aa31d698386e7e19849908d543`), with `LICENSE.txt` (the CC0 statement, source,
author, what was changed) and `README.md` (the geometry below). The FBX could not be used as it was: the kit vendors only
`GLTFLoader`, and three.js's FBXLoader does not read FBX 6.1 anyway. The conversion keeps every vertex and triangle;
the textures were shrunk from 4096² to 1024² (colour 185 KB, normal 306 KB as 4:4:4 JPEG) and 512² (the
occlusion/roughness/metallic map, 4 KB: it is nearly uniform). Checked by loading it with the kit's own GLTFLoader in a
local page and rendering stills (see "Verification" at the end).

What the scan is, judged from six textured renders and the texture itself:

- A real, complete shell: one closed surface with a single hole, the aperture (a rim loop of 105 vertices). The spire
  is on the −Y side in the GLB's frame, the aperture faces +Y tilted toward +Z (see README.md for the numbers). The
  surface is lumpy at the 3k-polygon level; the normal map carries the growth lines and the spire's whorls.
- Proportions: extents 1.000 × 0.873 × 0.755 after re-centring. The scanned shell is more depressed than a garden
  snail's: height/diameter about 0.75 against Cornu's 0.85–1.0 (25–35 mm high for 25–40 mm across). **Scale the
  coiling axis (apex to aperture) by 1.15–1.25 when wearing it.**
- Colour: a yellow ground with one broad dark-brown band and a yellow lip. That is a banded helicid (the file is
  tagged "forest" and named "Haus", German for a snail's shell; a Cepaea or Arianta is likely), not Cornu. Cornu's shell
  is "a reticulated pattern of dark brown, brownish-golden, or chestnut with yellow stripes, flecks, or streaks
  (characteristically interrupted brown colour bands)", its aperture "large and characteristically oblique, its margin
  in adults is whitish and reflected" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornu_aspersum)); the 2026 Colombian
  study identified its snails by "the typical light-brown coloration and the presence of characteristic dark spiral
  banding consisting of five brown bands surrounding the shell"
  ([Pardo-Sarmiento et al. 2026](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0354962)).
  **Repaint the colour** (the site's rule also bars a saturated yellow: browns and buff only): keep the scan's
  geometry, normal map and occlusion, and paint the Cornu pattern procedurally, as `snail.js` already does for its
  parametric shell (buff ground, up to five interrupted chestnut bands broken by pale flecks, a whitish reflected lip,
  a worn pale apex), either in UV space or by 3D position.
- The texture is one planar UV island: texel density varies twenty-fold across the mesh (UV area / surface area from
  0.024 to 0.78), so the far side of the shell is smeared. Another reason to repaint rather than re-grade.

Verdict: **good enough to build on for the shell's shape and surface relief; not for its colour.** If the builder
prefers the fully parametric shell already in `snail.js` (a clean logarithmic spiral with the right whorl count, 4.4
turns), that is defensible too; the scan's gift is organic irregularity and true growth lines.

## 2. Motion

### 2.1 What the record offers, and what it does not

- Muybridge's *Animal Locomotion* has no gastropod plates (its subjects are people, horses and other mammals and
  birds); the Macaulay Library "includes amphibians, fishes, and mammals" besides birds and names no invertebrates
  ([Macaulay Library, About](https://www.macaulaylibrary.org/about/)). The motion facts below come from the
  experimental literature instead.
- The two classic kinematic sources are H. W. Lissmann (1945), *The mechanism of locomotion in gastropod molluscs I.
  Kinematics*, J Exp Biol 21:58–69 (https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/21/1-2/58/9452/; paywalled, abstract
  only read: Helix shows direct monotaxic waves), and Lai, del Álamo, Rodríguez-Rodríguez & Lasheras (2010), *The
  mechanics of the adhesive locomotion of terrestrial gastropods*, J Exp Biol 213:3920–3933
  (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6514465/), which measured Helix aspersa (= Cornu aspersum) among four
  species.

### 2.2 The glide (adhesive crawling)

Facts:

- Mechanism: "a train of periodic muscle contractions (pedal waves) and relaxations (interwaves) that propagate from
  their tails to their heads", acting on a mucus layer; the measurements are "consistent with the surface of the
  ventral foot being lifted with the passage of each pedal wave", with "vertical displacements up to 80 μm"; "the
  interwaves remain stationary in the laboratory reference frame"; "wavelength increased steadily with a constant
  acceleration as the pedal waves propagated from the posterior end and reached a maximum value after traveling ∼80% of
  the animal's length"; the animals studied spanned "6–23" pedal waves at a time; wave speed was "2.61±0.44 and 4.54±1.68
  times the animal's crawling speed" in the two slug species; crawling speed "correlated positively with the mean
  wavelength as well as wave frequency" ([Lai et al. 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6514465/), at
  20–22 °C).
- In the garden snail itself the waves run down a central band of the sole and a smooth rim glides around it: the rim
  was "40.18±1.85%" of the sole's area (Table 1), "The wave trains on the ventral surface of garden snails appeared to
  be wider compared to those observed in slugs", and the stress under the anchored interwaves was 2.39±0.30 times that
  under the waves ("N=7 runs, 3 animals") ([Lai et al. 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6514465/)). So
  the foot's visible edge never ripples; only the sole's middle does, and only from below.
- Waves are direct (travelling the way the animal goes) and monotaxic (one wave spans the whole width of the sole) in
  pulmonates; "crawling velocities typically between 1 mm/s and 1 cm/s"; the mucus layer is "on the order of tens of
  microns thick" ([Lauga & Hosoi 2006](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0608363), summarizing Lissmann 1945 and Denny).
- Speed of Cornu itself, on smooth horizontal surfaces (adhesive crawling): "The mean speed of the snails using
  adhesive crawling on glass was 0.20 cm/s (SD 0.08)"; on acrylic 0.19 cm/s (SD 0.07); the same snails on concrete
  crawled at 0.14 cm/s before switching to loping at 0.15 cm/s; "There were no clear effects of snail size (as shell
  length) on speed"; snails 11.75–29.35 mm shell length, 0.41–6.9 g, at 18–22 °C
  ([McKee, Voltzow & Pernet 2013](https://www.originalwisdom.com/wp-content/uploads/bsk-pdf-manager/2020/08/McKee-et-al_2013_Substrate-Attributes-Determine-Gait-in-a-Terrestrial-Gastropod.pdf),
  Biol Bull 224:53–61). On a treadmill at 21 °C: "Snail speed ranged from 0.02 cm·min⁻¹ to 12.3 cm·min⁻¹ (median±IQR
  = 3.3 ± 3.9 cm·min⁻¹)", i.e. a median of 0.55 mm/s and a top of 2.05 mm/s
  ([Pardo-Sarmiento et al. 2026](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0354962)).
  Substrate texture and orientation change the speed strongly, body size and a conspecific's trail do not
  ([Pembury Smith & Ruxton 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/jzo.12865), J Zool 314:12–19; the open copy at
  https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/handle/10023/24786 answered 503 today, so only the abstract was read).
- Within Cornu, longer feet make longer waves, and on a horizontal surface wave frequency does not depend on foot
  length; on a vertical surface frequency falls with foot length and heavier snails are slower
  ([Hemmert & Baltzley 2016](https://doi.org/10.1086/BBLv230n1p78), Biol Bull 230:78–84, abstract).
- The second gait, loping: on dry porous surfaces (concrete, wood) "the head is periodically lifted well off the
  substrate, extended forward, and then returned to the substrate. This creates an arch in the foot over the
  substrate", several arches at once, which "do not move relative to the substrate"; 23 of 24 snails began loping after
  2–9 cm of ordinary crawling on concrete; none loped on glass or acrylic
  ([McKee et al. 2013](https://www.originalwisdom.com/wp-content/uploads/bsk-pdf-manager/2020/08/McKee-et-al_2013_Substrate-Attributes-Determine-Gait-in-a-Terrestrial-Gastropod.pdf)).
  A web page is glass: the companion crawls adhesively and never arches, but the head-lift-and-reach is a real movement
  the dance can borrow.

### 2.3 The tentacles (the eyestalks and the lower pair)

Facts:

- "The head bears four tentacles; the upper two are larger and bear eye-like light sensors, and the lower two are
  tactile and olfactory sense organs"; the snail "extends the tentacles by internal pressure of body fluids, and
  retracts all four tentacles into the head by invagination when threatened or otherwise retreating into its shell"
  ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornu_aspersum)). "Snails which are supplied with a hydro skeleton have no
  extensor muscles, instead the haemolymph pressure plays an extensor function" ([Hernádi & Teyke 2013](https://real.mtak.hu/9279/1/Hernadi_Teyke_kezirat.pdf),
  Cell Tissue Res 352:217–225, citing Chase 2002). The "nose" (the olfactory pad) is "situated at the superior
  tentacle's tip" ([Prescott, Gill & Chase 1997](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.1997.78.6.2951)).
- How a protracted tentacle moves: by "bending the tentacle around a basal pivot in different directions without
  curving the stem", pulled by three thin flexor muscles anchored at the base (M1 ventromedial, M2 ventrolateral, M3
  dorsolateral) that work against the haemolymph pressure; a separate retractor muscle, joined to the columellar
  muscle, draws the tentacle in; "local movements of the tentacle tip" are a third kind of movement
  ([Hernádi & Teyke 2013](https://real.mtak.hu/9279/1/Hernadi_Teyke_kezirat.pdf)). The tip's own movements are a
  lateral "quiver" driven by motor neurons inside the tentacle ([Hernádi et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10158-014-0170-8),
  abstract), the "twitching and quivering" of Lemaire & Chase (1998, J Comp Physiol A 182:81–87; title only).
- Postures, from Peschel, Straub & Teyke (1996, J Comp Physiol A 178:317–327) as reported by Hernádi & Teyke 2013: "i)
  the upright position during locomotion ii) external lateral movements of the upright tentacle during exploration,
  iii) external lateral downward position during the perception of the conditioned odor, iv) the forward horizontal
  position during approaching the conditioned food"; "during normal locomotion both naïve and food conditioned snails
  kept the posterior tentacles upright"; a snail that smells its food "lowered the ipsilateral tentacle pointing at the
  direction of food. At the same time the contralateral tentacle remained in upright position. During the final
  approach to the food both tentacles were oriented horizontally pointing directly at the food."
- "the protracted tentacles performed continuous scanning, both spontaneously and in response to odours. Odour
  applications elicited a brief startle-like quiver of the tentacle in a concentration-independent manner as well as a
  concentration-dependent contraction" (Helix lucorum; [Nikitin et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1460-9568.2005.04480.x),
  abstract). Length is graded: the motor neuron MtC3 "produces tentacle contraction" and regulates "the scanning area by
  regulating the tentacle length".
- The withdrawal reflex to a touch "involves bending and retraction of the tentacles"; "the excitation caused by a
  brief stimulus is transformed into a prolonged neuronal discharge" ([Prescott et al. 1997](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.1997.78.6.2951)).
  In the isolated preparation the response's "rising phase" was read at 2 s, its "peak phase" at 7 s and its "late
  phase" over 15–30 s after a 1 s stimulus, and "the first 30 sec of each response were recorded"; repeated weak
  stimuli habituate (every phase shrinks), strong ones also sensitize (the late phase grows)
  ([Prescott & Chase 1999](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC311297/), Learn Mem 6:363). The learned
  "tentacle lowering" toward a food odour is the standard conditioned response in Cornu
  ([Muñiz-Moreno & Loy 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.beproc.2023.104889), abstract).

### 2.4 Head and neck

- Adhesive crawling keeps the head on the ground, the front edge of the sole leading. Loping lifts it: "the head is
  periodically lifted well off the substrate, extended forward, and then returned to the substrate"
  ([McKee et al. 2013](https://www.originalwisdom.com/wp-content/uploads/bsk-pdf-manager/2020/08/McKee-et-al_2013_Substrate-Attributes-Determine-Gait-in-a-Terrestrial-Gastropod.pdf)).
- Numbers (judgement, scaled from that description): the head rises 0.15–0.25 units and reaches 0.1–0.2 units forward
  over 0.8–1.2 s, holds 0.3 s, sets down over 0.6 s. Neck yaw ±30°, pitch −10° to +35°. Use it for `notice` (rise
  toward the person) and once in the dance; never during the plain glide on the page.
- The lower tentacles touch the ground ahead as the snail moves (tactile and olfactory organs: Wikipedia). Judgement
  for the look: they tap alternately, 0.5–1 Hz, each tap 0.3 s, a tell-tale a viewer recognises at once.

### 2.5 Breathing, pulse, and the "always breathing" rule

- The breathing pore (pneumostome) "is an opening in the right side of the mantle"; "The frequency of pneumostome
  closing and opening is typically less than 0.5 closures per minute in fully hydrated slugs and snails. The rate of
  closures per minute increases the more dehydrated the slug is" ([Wikipedia, Pneumostome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumostome),
  citing Dickinson, Prior & Avery 1988, Comp Biochem Physiol A 89:579–585). On the companion it sits under the shell's
  lip on the snail's right, a dark oval 0.04–0.06 units across when open (size judgement).
- Heart: "heart rate significantly varied between retracted (mean±s.e.m = 32.7 ± 1.7 BPM) and moving states (mean±s.e.m
  = 42.0 ± 2.0 BPM)"; "the heart can be seen through the shell with proper lighting"
  ([Pardo-Sarmiento et al. 2026](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0354962)).
- Numbers: pneumostome cycle = open over 1.5 s, stay open 20–60 s, close over 1 s, closed 60–180 s (so 0.3–0.5
  cycles/min, hydrated); make it faster (once a minute) when the snail is "ducked" or has just reacted. The continuous
  "breath" the Field Guide requires is a slow swell of the mantle collar and the upper body of 1–2 % at 6–8 cycles/min
  (judgement; the lung ventilates through the pore, so it is small and slow), plus, if the shell is drawn translucent
  near the apex, a 0.6–0.7 Hz pulse (36–42 bpm) of 1 % brightness: the real heartbeat.

### 2.6 Weight, the shell, slopes

- The shell is about 11 % of the animal's mass (Pardo-Sarmiento: medians 0.38 of 3.48 g). It is carried on the back
  over the rear half of the foot by the columellar muscle, to which the tentacle retractors also attach
  ([Hernádi & Teyke 2013](https://real.mtak.hu/9279/1/Hernadi_Teyke_kezirat.pdf)). Carriage angle and rocking: no
  measured source. Judgement: apex up, back and to the snail's right (dextral), the aperture down-forward-left on the
  mantle; during the glide the shell rides level with a slow roll of ≤ 2° at 0.1–0.2 Hz, out of phase with the head's
  movements; when the head lifts (2.4) the shell tips back 3–5° and settles with a damped overshoot of 0.3 s.
- A snail on a tilted surface turns to head uphill (negative geotropism) after a measurable delay: "leads to negatively
  geotropic orientation after a measurable latent period or reaction time. The duration of the latent period is a
  function of the slope of the surface... On slopes up to 55 degrees, the relation between latent period and the sine
  of the slope is hyperbolic; above this inclination, the latent period sharply declines"; loads "up to 1/3 of its own
  mass" on the shell did not change this ([Hoagland & Crozier 1931](https://doi.org/10.1085/jgp.15.1.15), J Gen Physiol
  15:15); the path angle on the slope follows the slope's steepness ([Crozier 1935](https://doi.org/10.1085/jgp.18.5.659),
  J Gen Physiol 18:659, on Helix nemoralis). For the dock: every turn begins late (0.5–1.5 s after the cause) and is
  slow; nothing about a snail is snappy except the tentacles pulling in.

### 2.7 Into the shell, and out again

- The body is "able to be retracted entirely into the shell, which the animal does when inactive or threatened"
  ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornu_aspersum)). Boldness in Cornu is measured as the "latency to exit
  the shell following a simulated attack", which is repeatable per individual; "Subadults were bolder and more
  dispersive than adults" ([Dahirel et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1101/098822), abstract; the latencies themselves
  are in the paywalled paper and were not read).
- Judgement for the look: order of withdrawal is tentacles (0.3 s), head and neck (0.6 s), then the foot folding in
  from the rear (1.5–3 s); emergence is the reverse, far slower, the tentacles coming out last and unrolling (eversion)
  over 2–4 s. The dock's `react` only ever does the first step; the full withdrawal is for a future "long rest" if
  wanted, and the `rest` clip should show the stalks half-drawn and the head lowered, as it does now.

## 3. The hover dance: "the semaphore" (2.2 s)

Built only from movements the real animal makes: the loping head-lift (McKee), tentacle scanning and the food-approach
convergence (Peschel/Hernádi), the tip quiver (Lemaire & Chase; Nikitin), the shell settling on its muscle, and one
breath through the pore. Nothing hops: a snail has no feet to hop with. Times are seconds from the start; p = t/2.2.
Every channel returns to zero by the end, as the kit requires.

| Time | What happens | Channels (rig units, degrees) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.00–0.25 | Startle-quiver: both eyestalks shorten 20 % and quiver sideways, out of phase | stalkL/R `sy` −0.20 (bump 0–0.25), `rz` ±6° at 9 Hz × env |
| 0.25–1.05 | The head lifts and reaches toward the viewer, as in loping; the shell tips back and the body behind it rises a little | head `py` +0.20, `pz` +0.15, `rx` −25° (smooth in 0.5 s, hold 0.3); shell `rx` −4°, `py` +0.02; body `sy` +0.02 |
| 0.45–1.30 | Semaphore: the stalks sweep outward and back, alternately, big and slow, then converge forward-horizontal at the viewer (posture iv) | stalkL `rz` +35° then −10°, stalkR `rz` −35° then +10° (half a cycle apart, 0.4 s each); both `rx` −60° by 1.30 |
| 1.05–1.60 | The head sets down with weight; the shell overshoots 2° and settles | head `py` → 0 with a 0.3 s damped overshoot; shell `rx` → +2° → 0 |
| 1.30–1.90 | The stalks come back upright and re-extend; one tip gives a last quiver | stalkL/R `rx` → 0 (keys), `sy` +0.06 then 0; stalkL `rz` ±4° at 8 Hz for 0.25 s |
| 1.60–2.20 | The breathing pore opens and closes once (a puff) while everything settles | pneumostome open 0 → 1 → 0 (bump 1.6–2.2) |

Variant for a second hover: mirror left and right, and let the head turn 15° toward the pointer's side.

## 4. The other clips, in the same terms

- `arrive` (1.8 s): a snail cannot cross the dock in 1.8 s at 2 mm/s (that would take 15 s), so the arrival is a
  short, honest glide: enter from 0.5 units off, at 0.28 units/s (a stated time-lapse), 10 waves on the sole at 2 Hz,
  stalks half-drawn at first and extending as it settles, lower tentacles tapping the ground. Alternatively arrive
  "already here" and only extend the stalks.
- `idleA` (6 s): the mantle swell, one pneumostome cycle if due, the stalks' slow independent scanning (±15°, 0.15 Hz).
- `idleB` (3.6 s, the species fidget): a scanning bout: one stalk lowers 40° to the side and the other stays up
  (posture iii), then both return; the lower tentacles tap; one tip quiver.
- `notice` (1.8 s): the head lifts 0.15 units toward the person and both stalks converge forward (posture iv); eyes on
  them; back down with weight.
- `react` (1.8 s): the withdrawal reflex as in 2.3, head bending down with it; a second tap within 10 s gets a
  smaller one (habituation).
- `talk` (3 s loop): head raised a little and facing the person, stalks forward-horizontal with small independent
  nods (±8°, 0.5 Hz), the "pointing at the food" posture: attentive without being a cartoon.
- `lookLeft` / `lookRight` (1.6 s): the whole head yaws 30–45° and both stalks bend the same way around their bases;
  the far stalk shortens 10 % (it is turning away from the light).
- `rest` (6 s loop): stalks drawn to 55 %, head lowered to the ground, breath slower, pore cycling once a minute.

## Verification of the model file

The GLB was loaded with the kit's own `vendor/jsm/loaders/GLTFLoader.js` in a local page served from the session's
scratchpad (nothing published), rendered from four directions in the dock's lighting, textured and as clay, and the
stills were read as PNGs. Result: see the line below, written after the check.

Result (2026-10-03): the loader reported "3088 vertices, 6069 triangles; box 1.000 x 0.873 x 0.755", the material
bound all four maps ("map=true normalMap=true roughnessMap=true aoMap=true", metalness 0, roughness 1), the `extras`
(aperture and apex) came through, and the eight stills (textured and clay, from +Z, +X, +Y and −Y) showed a complete
globose shell with its growth lines in relief and the low spire's whorls. Two things the stills make plain for the
builder: seen into the aperture the shell is hollow and single-sided (set the shell material `side: DoubleSide`, or
let the sculpted mantle fill the opening, as the current `snail.js` does with a dark disc), and the yellow band must
be repainted. The lab page and server lived only in the session's scratchpad and were not published.
