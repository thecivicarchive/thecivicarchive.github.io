# Nickname candidates, waiting for John's approval

The site leads with the name the official record gives a bill. Names that are in wide use but are **not** in the
record can also be shown, as "commonly called ...", once John approves them one by one. Nothing on this page is on
the site yet.

**How to approve:** tell Claude the numbers you approve ("approve 2, 4 and 7"), and for each one whether the common
name should **lead** (replace the headline, with the official name beneath) or ride **beneath** the official name.
Approved names go into `nicknames.json` with their source link; everything else stays here.

Every link below came back from a web search on 2026-09-20 and shows the name in actual use. Descriptions in
quotation marks are the wording the source itself uses.

## Things to weigh

- **2 and 3 are descriptions more than nicknames.** Nobody says "the bill that ended the shutdown" as a name, but it
  is how the bill is described everywhere and the official titles are close to unreadable. If "commonly called"
  feels too strong for these, the alternative is to leave them to the plain-language summary on the card.
- **9 is worded with care.** The sponsor calls it a "stock trading ban"
  (https://steil.house.gov/media/press-releases/house-passes-steil-s-congressional-stock-trading-ban); critics say
  it is not one, because members keep the stock they hold
  (https://www.newsweek.com/house-passes-the-stop-insider-trading-act-but-trump-is-exempt-12232811). "Stock trading
  bill" takes neither side.
- **H.R. 5334 has changed since your last refresh.** The White House says it was signed on September 18, 2026
  (https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/2026/09/congressional-bill-h-r-5334-signed-into-law/). Your data
  ends September 17 and still shows it on the President's desk; the weekly refresh will move it to law.
