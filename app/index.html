The app

Keep it on your phone.

The Civic Archive can live on your phone, tablet or computer as an app. It is this website, installed from the website itself: free, with no app store and no account, and it updates itself whenever you are online.

You can keep this site on your phone or computer as an app. It is free. There is no app store and no account. It updates by itself.

How to get it

Press the button below and it shows the steps for the browser you are using now. Where the browser can install in one step, you confirm in its own box. Every step for every device is also written out here.

Press the button. It shows the steps for your phone or computer. All the steps are also below.

iPhone and iPad

  1. Open this site in Safari (or Chrome) and tap Share . In Safari on an iPhone, if you do not see it, tap the More button (three dots) beside the address first; on an iPad it is at the top.
  2. Scroll down the list and tap Add to Home Screen , then Add.

Android phones and tablets

  1. In Chrome, tap the menu at the top right, then Add to Home screen, then Install. Some versions say Install app.
  2. In Samsung Internet, tap the menu at the bottom right, then Add page to, then Home screen.
  3. In Firefox, tap the menu, then Add to Home screen.

Windows, ChromeOS and Linux computers

  1. In Chrome or Edge, click the install button at the right end of the address bar, then Install.
  2. No button there? In Chrome: the menu, then Cast, save and share, then Install page as app. In Edge: the menu, then Apps, then Install this site as an app.

Mac

  1. In Safari 17 or later: File, then Add to Dock, then Add.
  2. In Chrome or Edge: as on Windows, above.

Firefox on a computer does not install sites as apps the way other browsers do (newer versions on Windows can pin a site to the taskbar); use Chrome, Edge, or Safari on a Mac. A browser inside another app, such as a social media app, cannot either: open the page in Safari or Chrome first.

Firefox on a computer does not do it the same way. Use Chrome, Edge or Safari instead.

Part 2 of 6 · About 1 minute

What works with no connection

Pages you have opened on this device, and a ballot you kept, keep working with no connection. Anything you have not opened yet needs one. The app keeps up to about 60 MB; when that is full, the pages you opened longest ago make room for new ones. The Congress pages keep their own copies of the bills, votes and portraits you open, apart from that. Clear what is kept clears both; a kept ballot stays until you press Forget this ballot on its own page. Election Night’s figures always come from the network, so they show only when you are online. The scenes on the way in and the places to sit need a connection too.

Pages you have opened still work with no signal. So does a ballot you kept. New pages need a signal. Election Night needs a signal too.

Part 3 of 6 · About 1 minute

It updates itself

When you are online, every page comes fresh from the site, so the app is never behind the website. There is nothing to update by hand, and nothing to download from an app store.

When you are online, the app gets the newest pages. You never need to update it.

Part 4 of 6 · About 1 minute

Your privacy

Installing sends nothing to The Civic Archive. On Android, your browser’s maker (Google, for Chrome) builds the app from this site’s public description of itself; nothing you have read here is part of it. The app is this website, kept on your device. The pages it keeps stay on this device and are never sent anywhere. So that the note about the app appears only once, on your second visit, your browser keeps a count of your visits here; it never leaves this device either.

Getting the app sends nothing to this site. On Android, Google builds the app for Chrome from the site’s public details. What it keeps stays on your device.

Part 5 of 6 · About 1 minute

How to remove it

  • On an iPhone or iPad: touch and hold its icon, then tap Remove App.
  • On Android: touch and hold its icon, then tap Uninstall or Remove.
  • On a computer, from Chrome or Edge: open the app, then its menu, then Uninstall.
  • On a Mac, from Safari: drag it out of the Dock, then delete it from the Applications folder in your home folder.

On an iPhone, an iPad or a Mac, removing the app removes what it kept. With Chrome, Edge or Samsung Internet, the copies stay in the browser after the app is gone: press Clear what is kept above first, or clear this site’s data in the browser’s settings. Your reading settings stay in your browser until you clear them there.

On an iPhone or a Mac, removing the app removes what it kept. On other phones and computers, press Clear what is kept first.

Your kept ballot

On your state’s ballot page, “Keep this ballot on my phone” keeps your ballot on this device as a small app of its own, called My ballot. The app opens it too: it is one of the shortcuts on the app’s icon.

You can keep your ballot on your phone. It works with no signal. The app can open it.

Plain answers

Who pays for this site?

Nobody pays to appear on it. The site runs no advertising of its own and has no donors. It is published on GitHub's free public hosting.

Does this site track me?

This site counts nothing about its readers: no page sends a visit, a click or a place anywhere, except through the two choices below. There is no account and no sign-in. Your reading settings and your companion are kept in this browser, on this device, and are never sent anywhere.

Where a page offers "Use my location", your place is worked out on your device and is not sent anywhere. Every page takes its type from this site. Three things come from other servers. The opening picture of Plain Congress shows the weather at the Capitol in Washington, from Open-Meteo; that request carries the Capitol's place, never yours. If you switch on street maps on a ballot page, OpenStreetMap's servers see which map squares are asked for, and the page says so. And if you type your address on a ballot page and press Look up, your browser sends it straight to the U.S. Census Bureau's address finder, which sends back where it is; this site never sees the address or keeps it.

What happens when I share a page?

A Share button sends a link. On a phone it opens your phone’s own share sheet; on a computer, a short list of places, such as e-mail, Facebook or LinkedIn, or Copy link. Nothing is sent until you pick a place. The place you pick then sees the link and the short message with it. This site loads nothing from those services and never learns whom you share with. If a link would show roughly where you vote, the page says so first. The Access page says more.

Can I get it as an app?

Yes. You can keep this site on your phone, tablet or computer, installed from the site itself: free, with no app store and no account, and it updates itself. Pages you have opened, and a ballot you kept, still work with no connection. Installing sends nothing to this site; on Android, your browser’s maker (Google, for Chrome) builds the app from the site’s public description of itself. Get the app shows how.

Why should I trust the ratings?

You do not have to take them on trust. Facts come from official records and link back to them. Ratings are judgments, kept apart and labelled. Each one shows its evidence grade, its reasons, its sources and the version of the rubric it was made under.

So far ten bills carry a full set of ratings. They were applied by hand, by Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, as a preview, and each says so. Who backed a bill is worked out from the recorded votes by a published formula. The Method page has the whole rubric.
