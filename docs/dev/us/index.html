Congress, in plain words.

Thousands of bills move through Congress every year. Almost nothing reaches you unfiltered — it arrives as a press release, a cable segment, a fundraising email.

This place skips all of that. Every bill and every recorded vote, straight from the official record, written so you can follow it — and decide for yourself what you think.

        How the ratings work

        Facts come from the official record and are never edited. Ratings are judgments, and every one shows its evidence grade, its reasoning and the rubric version it was made under. The rater never sees who sponsored a bill or how the parties voted.

        1. Official record. Every bill, vote, cosponsor and summary is pulled from the Library of Congress feed each morning.
        2. Blinded rating. A model applies the published rubric to the summary and text, with sponsor and party removed.
        3. Human review. Every law and every bill with a floor vote is checked by a person; disagreements are published.
        4. Versioned. A re-score never erases the old rating. You can always see what changed and why.

          How this is worked out: a vote counts as a party split when most Democrats voted one way and most Republicans the other.           How to read this site

          Three kinds of claim appear here, and they are never mixed. Every one is labelled wherever it appears — so you always know whether you are looking at the record, at reasoning, or at a judgment.

          Fact

          From the record

          Bill text, sponsors, dates, committee actions, and every recorded vote member by member. Taken straight from GovInfo, the House Clerk and the Senate. If we have it wrong, the official source will say so — and we link to it every time.

          Analysis

          Derived, and shown

          Plain-language summaries, the ordering of lists like “Start here”, and party-backing figures worked out from roll calls. Built from facts by a stated rule, and the rule is always on the page so you can check it yourself.

          Opinion

          A judgment, labelled

          Ratings of who a bill helps and by how much. These are arguments, not measurements. Each one carries its evidence and its reasoning, and anything scored by machine says so until a person has reviewed it.

          Everything factual here comes from public records published by the United States government. Nothing on this site is edited by hand.
