Kansas, in plain words.

A state legislature writes much of the law closest to daily life: schools, roads, taxes, health care. Most of what reaches you about it arrives filtered — as a press release, a mailer, a headline.

This place skips the filter. Who represents you, what they work on, straight from public records — so you can decide for yourself what you think.

Who represents you?

Everyone in Kansas has one state senator and one representative. Find yours with your location, or pick your district if you know it. Your location is worked out on your device and never leaves it.

The shape of every district

Every Senate and House district in Kansas, measured the same way from the Census Bureau's own boundary files. A score describes a shape. It does not say why the shape is what it is: rivers, county and city lines, the state's borders and the Voting Rights Act all shape districts. This page measures, and leaves the judging to you.

Where this comes from

Everything factual on these pages comes from public records, and each part says which. Nothing is edited by hand, and nobody is characterized: the pages show the record and leave the judging to you.

How to read this site

Different kinds of claim appear here, and they are never mixed. Every one is labelled wherever it appears — so you always know whether you are looking at the record or at something worked out from it.
